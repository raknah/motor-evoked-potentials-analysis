## 0. Two things I got wrong first, and fixed

I am putting these first because they changed the numbers, and because if he finds them himself the
rest of the report loses credibility.

**(a) A geometry artifact was inflating every modulation depth.**
The transition-triggered histogram spans 3.5 stimulus cycles. My first version computed peak/trough by
folding that window modulo the stimulus period. 3.5 is not a whole number, so phases below 0.75 of a
cycle were covered four times and phases above it three times. A perfectly flat spike train folds to a
4:3 step and reads peak/trough = **1.33**. I verified this by folding uniform random numbers: 3.5 cycles
→ 1.34, whole numbers of cycles → 1.01.

That floor sat right in the middle of the real values, so it was not obviously wrong — pooled MUA read
MD ≈ 1.33–1.37 for *every* condition including ones with no locking at all, which is what gave it away.
The fix builds the cycle histogram from each spike's phase relative to its own preceding transition, so
every spike is counted exactly once. Corrected pooled-MUA MD for phase-reversing is **1.05**, i.e. no
modulation, which is the right answer. **All MD numbers below are post-fix.** VS, PPC and Rayleigh were
never affected — they were always computed from the per-spike phase.

**(b) My first harmonic control used the wrong test.** I originally asked "in what fraction of units is
the fundamental larger than the second harmonic?" and got 46 % at 40 Hz — a coin flip, because for an
unmodulated unit both are noise. That comparison is uninformative by construction. §5 now uses the
correct diagnostic.

**Guard against a repeat:** the notebook now runs three synthetic spike trains with known answers
through the identical estimator (§ "verification"). Flat → VS 0.002, PPC −0.00001, MD 1.06.
Strongly locked → VS 0.86, PPC 0.745. Two peaks half a cycle apart → VS 0.0004 despite obvious
structure. The third is the failure mode §5 is built to exclude.

## 3. Points 2 & 3 — contamination

**→ `figures/02_contamination.png`**

### 3.1 What KS `ContamPct` actually is

He was right to suspect it differs from the paper he linked. I read `kilosort/CCG.py` and
re-implemented it; the re-implementation reproduces `cluster_ContamPct.tsv` to within the file's
1-decimal rounding (max discrepancy 0.050), so this is not a guess:

$$\texttt{ContamPct} = 100 \times \frac{\min_{i\,\in\,1..10\,\text{ms}} R_i}{\max(R_{00},\,R_{01})},
\qquad R_i = \frac{K(|\tau| \le i)}{2\,i\,\Delta t\,N^2/T}$$

- $K$ = 1 ms-binned ACG, zero-lag bin excluded
- $R_i$ = observed refractory-window count ÷ count expected from a homogeneous Poisson train of the
  same mean rate — **this ratio is the firing-rate correction he is referring to**
- minimum taken over candidate refractory periods 1–10 ms, i.e. KS searches for the best-case window
- $R_{00}$, $R_{01}$ = the same density in the far (250–500 ms) and near (10–50 ms) ACG shoulders;
  dividing by their max re-references to the unit's *own* asymptote rather than to Poisson

So it is a **density ratio**: 0 % = clean, 100 % = refractory window as full as baseline, and **> 100 %
is possible and routine** — it means the unit bursts. Observed range here is 0–320 %, `mua` median
119 %. A "% contamination" above 100 % is not a fraction of anything, so it **cannot** be read as
"x % of these spikes came from another neuron".

Hill et al. 2011 — the paper he linked — estimates something different: the **false-positive fraction**,

$$f_p = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(1 - \sqrt{1 - \tfrac{2 N_{\text{viol}} T}{N^{2}(t_{\text{ref}} - t_{\text{cens}})}}\right)$$

bounded in [0, 0.5] and directly interpretable as "x % contamination". Both are in
`units_qc.csv`. They correlate (Spearman ρ = 0.87, p = 1.6 × 10⁻¹²) but are **not interchangeable**, and
a 10 % cut-off means different things in each — e.g. u25 reads ContamPct 1.8 % but $f_p$ 10.0 %.
7 of 45 good units saturate the Hill model (violations denser than any $f_p$ can explain).

`[inference]` My recommendation is to report **KS ContamPct** because it is what Kilosort actually used
to assign the `good`/`mua` labels, and to describe it as a refractory-violation density ratio rather
than as a contamination percentage.

### 3.2 "Is it the case that out of these 45 units about half are contaminated?"

**No — that was an artifact of my own threshold, and I can now say exactly why.**

The old plot used a metric I wrote for that notebook, not a standard:

$$\rho = \frac{\langle \text{ACG}(|\tau| \le 2.5\,\text{ms})\rangle}{\langle \text{ACG}(2.5 < |\tau| < 15\,\text{ms})\rangle}, \qquad \text{flag if } \rho > 0.1$$

with an eyeballed cut-off. That flagged **18/45 (40 %)** — the source of "about half".
KS `ContamPct` > 10 % (the Phy/IBL convention) flags **10/45 (22 %)**. The 10 are a strict subset of
the 18, so the disagreement is entirely the old rule over-flagging 8 units.

**Why it over-flagged.** Its denominator, the 2.5–15 ms window, is not a baseline — it is still inside
the unit's own post-spike dynamics. Measuring that window against the true far shoulder (250–500 ms):

| group | n | median (2.5–15 ms) / (250–500 ms) |
|---|---|---|
| flagged by neither | 27 | **3.70** |
| flagged by old rule only | 8 | **0.63** |
| flagged by both | 10 | 1.35 |

The 8 false alarms are units with a *wide post-spike suppression* — their 2.5–15 ms window is depressed
to 0.63× (as low as 0.04× for u128), so the denominator collapses and $\rho$ inflates even though there
are essentially no violations near zero lag. Conversely the 27 clean units are bursty, their 2.5–15 ms
window is 3.7× baseline, and the inflated denominator hides them. **The old metric was reading ACG
shape as much as contamination.** KS avoids this by referencing 10–50 ms and 250–500 ms, which are at
asymptote.

**Working set for everything below: 35 clean units (17 CTX, 18 HPC)** — `good`, ContamPct ≤ 10 %,

### 5.2 Metrics

His definition MD = FR_peak/FR_trough is reported. Two companions, because MD alone is not comparable
across these units:

- MD is a ratio of order statistics, so it is **biased upward and its floor depends on spike count**.
  From the synthetic check: a perfectly flat train reads MD **1.06** at 50 000 spikes but **1.31** at
  2 000. Units here differ in rate by 400×, so raw MD cannot be compared between them.
- `mod_depth_norm` = $(P-T)/(P+T) \in [0,1]$ — bounded, far more stable.
- **PPC** (Vinck et al. 2010, *NeuroImage* 51:112) — vector strength is itself biased upward at low
  spike count, and CTX and HPC differ systematically in rate, so raw VS would confound rate with
  locking. PPC is bias-free and is the column to use for any CTX-vs-HPC comparison.
  Empirically here VS vs rate: ρ = 0.49, p = 0.08 — the bias is present, which is why PPC is used.

### 5.4 Harmonic control — the obvious objection, excluded

The obvious artifactual reading of "weak at 40 Hz" is the **n:1 blind spot of vector strength**: a unit
firing twice per cycle at opposite phases has its two phase vectors cancel and reads VS ≈ 0 despite
perfect entrainment. This is live, not hypothetical — the TTL logs one edge per cycle (9600 events / 60
blocks / 4 s = 40 Hz) while a 40 Hz square-wave flicker physically has 80 transitions per second. My
synthetic check confirms the failure mode is real: a two-peak train reads VS 0.0004.

The correct diagnostic is not "which harmonic is bigger on average" — for an unmodulated unit that is a
coin flip, which is why my first attempt gave an uninformative 46 %. It is **whether any unit is
significantly locked at $2f_0$ while not locked at $f_0$**.

| condition | locked at f₀ only | locked at both | **locked at 2f₀ only** |
|---|---|---|---|
| flicker_40Hz | 5 (all CTX) | 0 | **0** |
| flicker_20Hz | 7 (6 CTX, 1 HPC) | 7 (all CTX) | **0** |

**Zero units, at either frequency, lock at the second harmonic without also locking at the
fundamental**, and VS_f₀ > VS_2f₀ in all 19 locked units. The 7 CTX units significant at both harmonics
at 20 Hz are simply showing the harmonic content of a sharp, non-sinusoidal peak.

**Conclusion:** the weak 40 Hz locking is a genuine failure to follow the flicker cycle by cycle, not a
cancellation artifact. `[established, within this session]`

`[inference]` This is consistent with the temporal low-pass behaviour of the early visual system, and
it does **not** contradict the 40 Hz amyloid literature (Iaccarino et al., *Nature* 540:230, 2016),
which rests on **LFP power**, not on spike phase-locking. It is worth flagging to him explicitly
because 20 Hz is nominally the negative-control frequency for the amyloid effect, and here it is the
frequency that drives cortical spiking hardest.

`[open]` I cannot turn preferred phase into a response latency. Locked CTX units cluster at 46–48 ms
of a 50 ms cycle, but phase is only defined modulo the period, the TTL marks an unknown edge, and the
photodiode is unusable (§8) — so display latency is unmeasurable. I would not put a latency number in
the email.

## 9. What I would and would not claim in the email

**Defensible from this session alone:**

- Cortical units follow 20 Hz flicker cycle by cycle (13/14 clean units, median PPC 0.023); hippocampal
  units do not (1/11).
- Cortical following at 40 Hz is present but much weaker (5/12, median PPC 0.0015); hippocampal
  following at 40 Hz is absent (0/12).
- This is not a vector-strength cancellation artifact — no unit locks at the second harmonic alone.
- Entrainment and firing-rate change dissociate: at 20 Hz, CTX rate changes are mixed in sign and the

**Not defensible yet:**

- Anything about response *latency* or absolute preferred phase (photodiode unusable).
- Anything about 5xFAD *vs* wild-type — there are no WT controls in these variables. This is
